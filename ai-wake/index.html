What counts

A follow-up paper is a new arXiv preprint whose main result builds on a document whose authors declare both its proofs and its writing generated by an AI model.

AI-generated works. A document counts as AI-generated when its authors, or its publisher, declare that an AI model generated both its proofs and its text. The list holds 663 works:

  • OpenAI’s October 6 release (github.com/openai/math, 2026), which says its manuscripts were “produced by an internal OpenAI model”: 722 manuscripts in 372 result families, each family counted as one work.
  • OpenAI’s other mathematics documents, each saying its proofs were produced by an OpenAI model: the disproof of Erdős’s unit distance conjecture (May 2026), the proof of the cycle double cover conjecture (July), “Ten advances in mathematics and theoretical computer science” (August; each of its ten results counted as one work), the improved lower bound for long gaps between primes, and the finite-time blow-up for the Navier–Stokes and for the Euler equations (September).
  • 175 arXiv papers of January to September 2026, in the categories below, whose statement on AI use says a model generated the text and also says a model produced the proofs or the main results. Papers whose statement covers only the writing, credits the model with a named part only (a key idea, an example), or leaves the extent of its part vague are not on the list. Papers of October 2026 join the list once the month closes.
  • 98 works on Hexagon, deposited in September and October 2026, whose depositor declares the text primarily AI-generated and whose provenance statement says a model produced the proofs, by the same reading as for arXiv papers.
  • Three documents added by hand: the manuscript constructing a complex structure on S6 that Claude produced under the direction of L. Alpöge; the proof of the Erdős–Sós conjecture (Erdős problem 548) found by GPT-6 Astra, with the write-up generated from it; and the argument improving the lower bound for gaps between primes (Erdős problem 4) posted on erdosproblems.com in August 2026, whose PDF names GPT 5.6 Sol as its author (the site credits GPT 5.6 Pro).

Follow-up papers. A paper follows up an AI-generated work when its main result, as its abstract and introduction present it, takes that work’s result as an input or a hypothesis; extends, improves or makes it explicit; adapts its method; or gives a new proof, an exposition, a formalization or a check of it. Papers that cite an AI-generated work for background, for comparison, to state priority, or in a remark are not counted; the table above gives their number. A paper sharing an author with the work it follows up is counted like any other.

The papers searched. Every new preprint from January 2026 to 22 October 2026 whose primary category is one of 28 arXiv mathematics categories: all of them except General Mathematics, History and Overview, Information Theory and Statistics Theory, with Mathematical Physics. Each paper is read from the source of its first version, and its title, abstract, comments and every source file are searched for the arXiv number, the Hexagon number, the title or the address of a work on the list. Each paper found was then read in full where it cites the work, and classified by Claude; every follow-up reading was checked against the paper’s text. Months are months of submission to August 2026, and of the arXiv number from September 2026.

Disclaimers

The counts record what papers say they do with an AI-generated work, and say nothing about whether any of the mathematics is right.

Not a judgement of correctness. A paper is listed for what it says it does with an AI-generated work: using it, extending it, explaining it, or checking it. Being listed says nothing about whether that paper or the AI-generated work is correct, and some follow-up papers were written precisely to check, or to correct, the work they follow.

Declarations are taken as stated. Whether a document is AI-generated is taken from what its authors or publisher declare; nothing has been verified. A document whose authors say nothing about AI is not on the list, however it was produced.

The counts are lower bounds. Only papers in the categories above are searched, only first versions are read, and a paper that refers to an AI-generated work without naming its arXiv number, title or address (“a recent AI proof of …”) is not found. October 2026 is incomplete, and its own AI-generated arXiv papers are not yet on the list.

Readings can be wrong. Each classification is a judgement made by reading the paper. Corrections are welcome; get in touch.
